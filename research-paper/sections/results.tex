\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Subject and Protocol}
We exercise \cs\ on \vellum, a retrieval-augmented-generation (RAG)
service written in Python; \cref{tab:subject} gives its profile. \vellum\
is a synthetic subject constructed to be structurally representative of a
production RAG system---document loader, semantic chunker, embedding
cache, hybrid dense/sparse retriever, cross-encoder reranker, guardrailed
generator, and an evaluation harness---and to contain the weakness
categories that the OWASP Top~10 for LLM
Applications~\cite{owaspllm2025} and the indirect-prompt-injection
literature~\cite{greshake2023,perez2022ignore} identify as
characteristic of this class. The measurements below therefore
characterise the platform's \emph{operational} behaviour---latency,
determinism, inter-modality agreement, and score sensitivity---which are
properties of the tool and its configuration rather than of any
ground-truth vulnerability labelling; a labelled-corpus accuracy study is
identified as future work in \cref{sec:discussion}.

\Cref{tab:setup} lists the hardware, model, and configuration.
Every timing and agreement metric is computed over $R=10$ repetitions;
we report the mean and, where noted, the standard deviation or the
$95$\textsuperscript{th} percentile.

\subsection{Findings and Modality Contribution}
A full audit surfaced $20$ fused findings. \Cref{tab:severity} breaks

representative subset with location, identifier, and originating
modality.

\input{sections/tables/tab-severity-hotspots}
\input{sections/tables/tab-findings}

Normalising by size gives a detection density
\begin{equation}
  D \;=\; \frac{N_{\text{findings}}}{\text{KLOC}}
    \;=\; \frac{20}{5.2} \;\approx\; 3.8~\text{findings/KLOC},
  \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
of which $1.3$~findings/KLOC are security-category. Of the $20$ fused
findings, $9$ were pattern-only, $4$ were model-only, and $7$ were
corroborated by both passes; the model thus contributed $11$ findings
that the rule set did or would surface, and the rules contributed $16$.

To quantify agreement we take as items the $48$ distinct
$(\textit{file},\,\textit{weakness-category})$ locations formed by the
union of the security findings' neighbourhoods and an equal number of
matched negative controls, and label each present/absent by the pattern
pass and by the model pass. \Cref{tab:agreement} is the resulting
contingency table. Chance-corrected agreement is Cohen's $\kappa$,
\begin{equation}
  \kappa \;=\; \frac{p_o - p_e}{1 - p_e}
         \;=\; \frac{0.896 - 0.615}{1 - 0.615}
         \;=\; 0.73,
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
where $p_o$ is the observed agreement and $p_e$ the agreement expected by
chance from the marginals. A value of $0.73$ is ``substantial'' on the
usual interpretation: the two modalities largely see the same things,
and the disagreements are where each contributes uniquely---the model on
a citation-integrity weakness no syntactic rule encodes, the rules on an
unsafe-deserialisation call the model under-weighted.

\subsection{Structural Hot-Spots}
The complexity engine flagged five functions above the default budget
(\cref{tab:hotspots}); \texttt{HybridRetriever.retrieve} is the worst at
$CC=26$. Mean cyclomatic complexity across the project is
$\overline{CC}=7.8$ over $214$ decision points---healthy on average, with
risk concentrated in a small number of methods, which is the signal the
ranking is meant to expose (\cref{tab:hotspots}).

\subsection{On-Device Model Pass: Latency}
\label{sec:eval-latency}
The per-file cost of the model pass is well described by an affine
function of the input length $L_f$ (code plus prompt, in tokens),
\begin{equation}
  T_{\text{llm}}(f) \;=\; \alpha + \beta\, L_f,
  \label{eq:tllm}
\end{equation}
with a least-squares fit over the $47$ files giving $\alpha = 0.83$\,s
(fixed prompt-load and decode set-up) and $\beta = 6.9$\,ms/token, with
$R^2 = 0.94$ (\cref{fig:evalplots}, left). Across the project the model
pass took a mean of $3.7$\,s per file (median $3.1$\,s, $p_{95}=8.6$\,s,
$\sigma = 2.4$\,s), for an aggregate of $174$\,s.

\subsection{End-to-End Audit Time}
The total audit time is the sum of the stage costs,
\begin{equation}
  T_{\text{audit}} \;=\; T_{\text{walk}} + T_{\text{pat}}
     + T_{\text{cx}} + \sum_{f \in F} T_{\text{llm}}(f)
     + T_{\text{dyn}}.
  \label{eq:taudit}
\end{equation}
Measured over $R=10$ runs, $T_{\text{audit}} = 196 \pm 11$\,s
($\approx 3.3$\,min), with the non-model stages---tree walk
($0.4$\,s), pattern scan ($1.2$\,s), complexity ($0.7$\,s), and the
dynamic pass ($19.3$\,s)---contributing $21.6$\,s in total. The model
pass is therefore $89\%$ of wall time. Because $T_{\text{llm}}$ is
independent per file, \cref{eq:taudit} is linear in $|F|$ with slope
$\overline{T}_{\text{llm}}\approx 3.7$\,s/file against a fixed cost of
$\approx 21.6$\,s; a $500$-file repository would take $\approx 31$\,min
on this hardware with a single model instance, and roughly
$5$--$10\times$ less with the model on a GPU. Crucially, and as
instrumented at the network interface, the entire audit issued
\emph{zero} outbound requests.

\subsection{Determinism}
With a fixed seed and temperature $0.1$, we ran the model pass $K=10$
times per file and measured the stability of the returned finding set by
the mean pairwise Jaccard index,
\begin{equation}
  \bar{J} \;=\; \frac{1}{|F|}\sum_{f\in F}
     \binom{K}{2}^{-1}\!\!\sum_{i<j}
     \frac{|A_{f,i}\cap A_{f,j}|}{|A_{f,i}\cup A_{f,j}|},
  \label{eq:jaccard}
\end{equation}
where $A_{f,i}$ is the finding set for file $f$ on run $i$. We obtain
$\bar{J}=0.94$, and the per-file finding count has a coefficient of
variation of $0.03$ (\cref{tab:determinism}). Re-auditing an unchanged
project therefore reproduces the same result up to occasional
rewording of a description, which matters for using the \phindex\ as a
regression gate.

\subsection{Composite Risk and Its Sensitivity}
Substituting the case-study values into
\cref{eq:sv,eq:sc,eq:sq,eq:h} with $n_{\text{sev}}=6$,
$\overline{CC}=7.8$, and $a=1$ gives $s_v = 40$, $s_c = 61$, $s_q = 95$
(\cref{tab:risk}), and
\[
  H = \operatorname{round}(0.5{\cdot}40 + 0.3{\cdot}61 + 0.2{\cdot}95)
    = \operatorname{round}(57.3) = 57,
\]
i.e.\ the \emph{moderate} band. Where $s_v,s_c \in (0,100)$ the score is
linear, so its sensitivity is constant:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial H}{\partial n_{\text{sev}}} = -5, \qquad
  \frac{\partial H}{\partial \overline{CC}} = -1.5, \qquad
  \frac{\partial H}{\partial a} = +9.
  \label{eq:sens}
\end{equation}
Each additional critical- or high-severity finding costs $5$ points of
$H$; each unit of mean complexity costs $1.5$; completing the audit
rather than aborting it is worth $9$. Holding $\overline{CC}=7.8$ and
$a=1$, the project remains in the moderate band while
$n_{\text{sev}}\le 7$ and crosses into \emph{critical} at
$n_{\text{sev}}=8$ ($H=47$): the case study is exactly two qualifying
findings away from the critical band, a concrete remediation target.
\Cref{tab:sensitivity} tabulates $H$ over a grid of $n_{\text{sev}}$ and
$\overline{CC}$, and \cref{fig:evalplots} (right) plots the case-study
slice with the band boundaries.
